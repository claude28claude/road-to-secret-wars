The Domes of Battleworld

Battleworld is not a place Doom finds — it is a place he builds. When the multiverse collapses, Doom salvages the pieces he wants and welds them into a single world, then writes himself in as its god. Every dome is a slice of a dead reality with its own laws, its own ruler, and no memory that anything else ever existed. Crossing between them without Doom’s leave is a capital crime.

Nothing below is confirmed MCU plot. The domes come from the 2015 Secret Wars comic, and each is graded honestly on the case for it turning up on screen: Locked in, Strong case or Wildcard. Tap a dome’s card to mark it briefed; open the row for the full dossier.

The rest of the map

Nineteen deep cuts

The 2015 comic mapped forty-one domains. These are the smaller, stranger ones — mostly there to prove the planet is bigger than any one story. Marvel could pull any of them out of the drawer for a single shot.

The Laws of Battleworld

How a stitched planet stays stitched — the rules of the comic, and the ones you will want in your head going into the film.

Law I

Doom is God, and everyone agrees

Not metaphorically. Doom rewrote himself into the position, and the rewrite is retroactive — nobody on Battleworld remembers a time when he was not divine. The horror of Battleworld is not that people are oppressed. It is that they are grateful.

Law II

The barons rule, until they don’t

Each dome has a baron who can wage war, police their population, run their own economy and pass the title to their children. That autonomy has a hard ceiling: Doom can remove, exile or kill any baron at any time, without explanation, and has.

Law III

The Thor Corps is the police

A Thor drawn from every dome on the planet, all of them worthy, all of them answering to Doom rather than to Asgard. They settle disputes between domes and, when a domain is judged an existential danger, they are authorised to erase it.

Law IV

Sheriff Strange keeps the law and the secret

Strange holds court under Doom’s eye. He is the only person permitted to remember the multiverse, which makes him the most powerful man on Battleworld and the loneliest. He uses the privilege to hide people.

Law V

No dome may touch another

Crossing a border without Doom’s leave is a capital offence, and the geography enforces it — Sentinel belts, oceans and machine-lands sit between the domains on purpose. Every dome believes it is the world.

Law VI

The Shield holds three things back

The Deadlands, Perfection and New Xandar — zombies, Ultron and the Annihilation Wave. Doom could not beat them, so he built a wall and staffed it. The southern rim of the planet is one long admission of failure.

Law VII

Nothing is allowed to remember

Every population on Battleworld is given a history that starts with Doom. The moment anyone recovers a memory of the world before, they stop being a citizen and become a problem — and problems are handled by the Thor Corps.

Law VIII

The whole thing runs on one lie

Doom did not save the multiverse alone, and the person who knows it is the person he is most afraid of. Battleworld is stable for exactly as long as that stays unsaid. Every version of this story ends when somebody says it out loud.

How this road was built

Every judgement call, stated plainly, so you can disagree with a specific one instead of the whole thing.

What is the relationship to The Doomsday Protocol?

The Doomsday Protocol is part one: 94 entries from Iron Man to Doomsday. This is part two. Part I here overlaps with the end of that list on purpose, so you can start here without losing your place; everything after it is new.

Why a rewatch, and not more new titles?

Because there is almost nothing new to watch. Marvel cleared 2027 of cinema releases entirely so that Doomsday’s ending is the only thing in your head when Secret Wars opens. The useful homework is remembering the stories Battleworld is built out of — so Part III is the twenty-three titles the domes are cut from, each linked to the domes it explains.

Is Battleworld actually in the film?

Nobody outside Marvel knows. The film shares its name with the 2015 comic that introduced the domes, and the Russos have pointedly refused to rule it in or out. That is why every dome carries a grade — Locked in means the film barely works without it, Strong case means the MCU has already built the pieces, Wildcard means it is possible and fun but unsupported. These are my judgement, not Marvel’s.

What do Essential, Recommended and Optional mean here?

Essential — the two-film story leans on it directly. Recommended — a dome, a character or a thread from it is expected to matter. Optional — you would miss a reference, nothing more. Comics are always Optional.

Why do some titles say “Not out yet”?

They have not been released. Dates are Marvel’s or Sony’s announced dates where they exist; where they do not, the row says so plainly and the runtime is a placeholder. Nothing reported-but-unannounced is presented as confirmed.

Where is my progress stored?

In this browser’s local storage, saved per title rather than by position — adding entries will never scramble what you have ticked. It never leaves your device. If you used the earlier version of this site, your old ticks were carried over automatically.

Clearing site data, private browsing, or switching browser or device will lose it. “Save to file” and “Load from file” move it between devices.

Install it as an app

This is an installable app, not just a bookmark. Installed, it opens full-screen with its own icon and works offline — the road, your ticks and every poster you have already seen are cached on your device.

Chrome, Edge or Brave on desktop: the install icon in the address bar, or the Install app button above. Android: three-dot menu, then “Install app”. iPhone / iPad: Share, then “Add to Home Screen”.

After Secret Wars

Whatever survives Battleworld becomes the new Marvel universe, and Marvel has been unusually open that the next era is a mutant era. Violet dots are dates the studio has confirmed. Grey dots are announced with no date, or not announced at all.

5 May 2028 · Cinemas

Untitled X-Men film

The first film after Secret Wars, and the MCU’s first proper X-Men film. Jake Schreier, who made Thunderbolts*, directs. Marvel confirmed the date at D23 in August 2026 and announced a cast native to the rebuilt universe — Christopher Abbott as Professor X, Maya Boyd as Storm, Inde Navarrette as Rogue, and Adam Driver as the villain.

28 July 2028 · Cinemas

Ghost Rider

Ryan Gosling as Johnny Blaze, directed by Shawn Levy from a Jonathan Tropper script. Announced at Comic-Con in July 2026 and dated at D23 a month later. Marvel’s clearest signal that the next phase goes darker and weirder.

15 December 2028 · Cinemas

Black Panther III

Ryan Coogler returns to close his trilogy, with David Jonsson as T’Challa’s son taking up the mantle. The only post-Secret Wars film that had a firm date from the day it was announced.

Announced · no date

Blade

Announced in 2019, rewritten repeatedly, still without a date or a shooting start. Mahershala Ali remains attached. Intended rather than imminent.

Announced · no date

Deadpool 4, Armor Wars, Nova, and a Shang-Chi sequel

All confirmed as in development, none dated. Armor Wars in particular has a dome-shaped hole waiting for it in Technopolis.

Not announced

Spider-Man 5 · Avengers 7

Neither exists officially. Both are treated as inevitable for obvious reasons, and both are here only because their absence from Marvel’s slate is itself notable.

Beyond

Phase Seven, whatever it turns out to be

Marvel has said the slate past Secret Wars is deliberately smaller and slower. Expect the map to be redrawn once Secret Wars has landed — which is the point of a film called Secret Wars.
